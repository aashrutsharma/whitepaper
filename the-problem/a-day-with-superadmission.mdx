Everything up to this point has been described piece by piece, one profile instead of four, one vault instead of four uploads, one dashboard instead of four tabs. This page puts it back together as one continuous session, the way Unnati would actually experience it.

She's still eligible for both a JoSAA-run seat through her JEE rank and a state quota seat through her state CET rank, exactly the situation from the Counselling page. This time, she's doing it with Superadmission.

<Steps>
  <Step title="She creates one profile">
    Unnati verifies her identity through an Aadhaar-linked OTP, the same way she'd log into most government services. Her basic details, name, date of birth, category, are pulled in and confirmed once. This profile is what every counselling system she connects to afterward will read from.
  </Step>
  <Step title="She connects both systems she's eligible for">
    She adds JoSAA and her state CET to her profile. Each connection asks for her specific consent, she's agreeing to share her verified information with that particular system, not handing over blanket access to everything.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Her documents get verified once">
    Her Class 12 marksheet and category certificate are fetched from DigiLocker where available. What isn't available there, she uploads once. Everything is checked a single time. Both JoSAA and her state CET see the same "verified" status, neither one re-runs the check independently.
  </Step>
  <Step title="She fills her preferences, with PraveshAI showing her the odds">
    As she builds her list of colleges and branches, PraveshAI shows a probability next to each one, based on her rank, her category, and how similar ranks landed in past years. She still decides the order. Nothing gets added or removed on her behalf.
  </Step>
  <Step title="She locks her list, with one dashboard tracking both deadlines">
    JoSAA's locking window and her state CET's happen to fall two days apart. Her dashboard shows both, sorted by which is closer, with a clear warning since the gap between them is tight enough to be risky if she isn't paying attention.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Both results land on the same screen">
    When allotments are released, she sees her JoSAA result and her state result side by side, instead of logging into two separate portals to piece it together herself.
  </Step>
  <Step title="She accepts one, and gets guided through withdrawing from the other">
    She decides to accept her JoSAA seat. Superadmission walks her through exactly what withdrawing from her state CET seat requires, since forgetting that step is one of the most common ways students accidentally lose deposits or lock themselves out of later rounds.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reporting day, in person, exactly as before">
    She still shows up to her institute physically, with her original documents, the same as any student would. The difference is what happens when she arrives: the institute scans a single code, and her payment, her allotment, and the absence of any duplicate admission elsewhere are all confirmed in seconds, instead of a staff member manually checking each one.
  </Step>
</Steps>

---

Nothing in this sequence removes a step JoSAA or her state CET actually requires. She still registers, still fills preferences, still gets allotted, still reports in person. What disappears is the part that was never really about admission in the first place, typing the same details in twice, uploading the same document twice, and finding out about a deadline collision only after it was too late to do anything about it.

That's the full picture of what changes for a student. The next question is a different one: how much of this can actually be trusted, and what happens legally to everything she just shared.
