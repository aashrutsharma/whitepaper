The overview page already made three promises. PraveshAI does not publish an allotment on its own. It does not make the final call on a document it is unsure about. It does not settle a grievance by itself. This page shows where those checkpoints actually sit, and why they sit in those places and not others.

## The rule we use

There is one rule for deciding where a person belongs in the process. If a decision could change whether a specific student gets a specific seat, a person reviews it before it becomes final. The review happens first, and only then does the decision count.

If a decision only removes repeated manual work, no person is needed. Checking that a document is in the right format is one example. Syncing a deadline across two dashboards is another. Nobody's outcome changes in those cases, and no judgment is being made that affects a student's seat. A checkpoint there would slow things down and protect no one, so we leave it out on purpose.

## Where a person steps in

  <Card title="Document verification" icon="contact">
    A document clears without a person only if it scores high on the automated check. If the system is less sure, the document goes to a reviewer, who makes the call instead of the system guessing. The scoring behind this is explained in the earlier sections on PraveshAI.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Publishing an allotment" icon="megaphone">
    A finished match never reaches a student automatically. It goes to the counselling authority first, and nothing is published until they give their explicit sign-off. The student only sees a result after a person has looked at it and approved it. [How a Counselling Authority Actually Runs a Round](https://docs.superadmission.com/operations/how-a-counselling-authority-actually-runs-a-round) covers this in full.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Grievances" icon="mail">
    A person resolves every dispute. Each one is sorted into a category and sent to whoever is actually in a position to decide it, so it does not sit with someone who cannot act on it. Nothing is ever closed automatically. [How a Dispute Actually Gets Resolved](https://docs.superadmission.com/operations/how-a-dispute-actually-gets-resolved) walks through the whole path.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Pausing, extending, or rolling back a round" icon="hand">
    These are the most disruptive actions the system allows, so each one needs explicit authorisation. A rollback goes one step further and needs more than one person to approve it. No single person can undo a round alone. [The Safety Levers Behind Every Round](https://docs.superadmission.com/operations/the-safety-levers-behind-every-round) explains how this works.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Why this matters more than it looks

A seat allocation decision shapes a real student's future. Once the student acts on it, it can be very hard, and sometimes impossible, to undo. Syncing a deadline reminder is nothing like that. The system treats the two differently on purpose, and that difference is the whole reason these checkpoints exist where they do.

This is also the reason PraveshAI is built to stay inspectable and not become a black box. It fits the Safe and Trusted AI focus of the IndiaAI Mission, which is the same frame Superadmission uses when it talks about being Digital Public Infrastructure. A system that makes decisions this serious has to be one where a person can always ask why and get a real answer, not just a result.
