The overview page for this section already said: PraveshAI doesn't publish an allotment on its own, doesn't make a final call on an uncertain document, and doesn't resolve a grievance by itself alone. This page is about where those checkpoints actually are, and why they are there specifically.

## The Principle

The rule used to decide where a human checkpoint belongs is simple: anywhere a decision could materially change whether a specific student gets a specific seat, a person reviews it before it becomes final. Anywhere a decision is just removing repeated manual work, checking a document's format, syncing a deadline across two dashboards, no outcome-changing judgment is being made, so no checkpoint is needed there.

## Why This Matters More Than It Might Seem

A seat allocation decision affects a real student's future, sometimes irreversibly, once they've acted on it. That's a fundamentally different kind of decision than syncing a deadline reminder, and it's treated differently on purpose. This is also the specific reason PraveshAI is built to stay inspectable rather than opaque, in line with the Safe and Trusted AI focus of the IndiaAI Mission, mentioned on Superadmission as Digital Public Infrastructure. A system making decisions this consequential has to be one where a person can always ask why, and get a real answer, not just a result.

That "real answer" part, how every decision, human or automated, gets recorded so it can actually be explained later.
