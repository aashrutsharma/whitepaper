The earlier pages in this section followed one student's path through counselling in a lot of detail. That's useful for understanding how the system actually works step by step, but it can leave a misleading impression that this is built around one kind of student. It isn't. This page is a quick, honest look at the actual range of people this has to work for, and where to go if you want the full case for any one of them.

---

## Students

Three situations show up constantly in the real data behind this design, and each one puts different pressure on how the system needs to work.

<CardGroup cols={1}>
  <Card title="The first-generation applicant" icon="signpost">
    Nobody at home has done this before. Every assumption an official portal makes about what a student already knows becomes a genuine obstacle, not a minor inconvenience.
  </Card>

  <Card title="The student eligible across multiple systems at once" icon="shuffle">
    Someone with both a JEE rank and a state CET rank has to track two sets of deadlines, two sets of documents, and two allotments, entirely by hand, with no system telling them when the two are about to collide.
  </Card>

  <Card title="The student in a low-connectivity area" icon="signal-slow">
    Every extra login, every re-upload, every portal that times out on a slow connection is a real cost here, not a small annoyance. Design decisions that look trivial from a fast connection in a city can be the actual reason someone drops out.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Across roughly 2,000 real student admission journeys studied while building this, a consistent pattern holds: a meaningful share of students who start the process never finish it, and it's rarely because they weren't eligible. It's a missed deadline, a confusing document rejection, or simply never finding out a seat had opened up in time.

This is why one profile, one document vault, and one dashboard aren't built around a single ideal student. They're built to hold up for whichever of these three situations a given student is actually in.

## Institutions

Institutions currently absorb a lot of work that has nothing to do with actually admitting anyone, chasing incomplete records, correcting mismatches, re-verifying a document that was already checked somewhere else. What changes for them, including the real numbers on where their admissions staff's time actually goes today, is covered in full on **What This Looks Like for the People Running It**.

## Counselling Authorities

An authority's biggest time cost during a round is rarely the actual seat-matching decision, it's everything happening around it. What changes for them is covered on the same page above. Exactly how their systems plug in day to day is covered next, on **Authority Workflows**.
